Health & Wellness

Fall is the ideal time to schedule your family's annual flu shots — the CDC recommends getting vaccinated by the end of October for the best protection through peak season. Call your pediatrician or check local pharmacy walk-in clinics for availability.

As kids head back into fall sports and outdoor activities, now is a good moment to review cold-weather layering habits — dressing in moisture-wicking base layers and adding insulation as temperatures drop in the evenings helps prevent chills after practice. As always, consult your child's doctor with any specific health questions.

With days getting shorter after the fall equinox, it's worth keeping an eye on your family's mood and sleep routines — reduced daylight can affect energy levels and sleep quality for kids and adults alike. Getting outside for even 20 minutes of natural light each morning can make a noticeable difference. Talk to your doctor if you have concerns about seasonal mood changes.

Money-Saving Tips for Families

Ohio occasionally runs a back-to-school sales-tax holiday on clothing and school supplies in late summer or early fall — rules and eligible items can change year to year, so check the Ohio Department of Taxation's website for current-year details before your next shopping trip.

Read more →

The Cuyahoga County Public Library's museum pass lending program lets cardholders borrow free or discounted passes to area attractions — a fantastic way to enjoy fall outings like nature centers or children's museums without paying full admission.

Read more →

Fall is prime season to buy kids' summer clothing and gear at deep clearance discounts — many retailers mark down swimwear, shorts, and sandals significantly in September and October. Buying a size up now means your kids are set for next summer at a fraction of the cost.

Local Buy Nothing groups (searchable on Facebook by your Strongsville neighborhood) are a great way to score outgrown kids' clothing, toys, and sports gear for free — and to pass along items your own kids have grown out of before the new school year is too far along.
