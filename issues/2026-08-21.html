Health & Wellness

Late August heat is still very real in Northeast Ohio — while we all know to stay hydrated, this is also a good week to pay attention to the signs of heat exhaustion versus heat stroke in kids, who can overheat faster than adults during outdoor play. Pale skin, heavy sweating, and dizziness call for rest and cool fluids; confusion, hot dry skin, or loss of consciousness require a 911 call immediately. When in doubt, always consult your pediatrician or family doctor.

With back-to-school just around the corner, now is a great time to check whether your kids are up to date on recommended vaccinations before classrooms fill up again — Ohio school requirements are listed on the Ohio Department of Health website, but your child's doctor is the best person to review what's needed for their age. Getting this squared away in late August avoids the September rush.

Read more →

August is peak tick season in Ohio, especially in wooded and grassy areas — including local trail spots like Mill Stream Run Reservation. After any outdoor time, do a full tick check on kids (and pets) before coming inside, paying special attention to the scalp, behind the ears, and behind the knees. If you find an attached tick, remove it with fine-tipped tweezers and contact your doctor if a rash or fever develops in the following weeks. As always, consult your own physician for personal health guidance.

Money-Saving Tips for Families

Ohio typically runs a back-to-school sales-tax holiday in late summer that can cover clothing, school supplies, and sometimes school instructional materials — the exact dates, eligible items, and price caps change year to year, so check the Ohio Department of Taxation's website for this year's specific rules before you shop rather than relying on last year's details.

Read more →

The Cuyahoga County Public Library's museum pass lending program lets cardholders borrow free or reduced-admission passes to area attractions — including children's museums, science centers, and nature venues — at no cost. It's one of the most underused family perks in the area, and a great way to stretch your entertainment budget heading into fall.

Read more →

If the kids have outgrown summer clothes and you're stocking up for fall, check out Buy Nothing groups on Facebook (search your Strongsville neighborhood) or apps like ThredUp Kids and Kidizen for gently used school-year clothing at a fraction of retail prices — especially useful right now when back-to-school demand drives up prices at traditional stores.

Before buying new school supplies at full price, check Signal Cleveland's roundup of back-to-school supply giveaway events happening across Greater Cleveland — several organizations distribute free backpacks and supplies to families in need, and some events are open to all income levels. A quick search this week could save a meaningful amount before the first day of school.
